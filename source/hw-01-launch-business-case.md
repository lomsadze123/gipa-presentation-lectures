# **Homework 1: Apple — Launch the iPhone Flip**

**Scenario: You are Apple's management team. Apple is considering launching a new foldable smartphone called the iPhone Flip. Your task is to prepare a presentation convincing the CEO to approve your launch plan.**

**Presentation: 10 slides, 8–10 minutes.**

### **Slide 1 — Product announcement**

**Create a professional product launch slide.**

**Include the product name, an image or mockup of the iPhone Flip, a creative slogan, and the names of your team members.**

### **Slide 2 — Introduce your product**

**Explain the proposed product in detail.**

**Include at least four features, three reasons it is different from a regular iPhone, and one feature you believe will be its strongest selling point.**

### **Slide 3 — Why should Apple launch it?**

**Present three business reasons Apple should introduce this product.**

**Explain how it could help Apple increase revenue, expand its product portfolio, or strengthen its competitive position. Include one possible disadvantage of launching it.**

### **Slide 4 — Pricing strategy**

**Apple is considering three prices for the phone.**

| Option | Selling price |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Premium** | **\$1,800** |
| **Standard** | **\$1,500** |
| **Affordable** | **\$1,200** |

**Manufacturing and delivery costs total \$900 per phone.**

**Calculate the contribution per phone and contribution margin percentage for each price. Choose one pricing strategy and justify your decision.**

### **Slide 5 — Production and costs**

**Assume Apple plans to manufacture 400,000 phones.**

**Calculate the total variable production cost, total sales revenue using your selected price, and total contribution before fixed costs.**

**Present your calculations in a table or chart.**

### **Slide 6 — Break-even analysis**

**Apple must spend \$150 million on product development and launch activities.**

**Calculate how many phones Apple needs to sell to cover this fixed investment using your selected price.**

**Show the formula, your calculations, and a break-even chart.**

### **Slide 7 — Profit challenge**

**Apple wants to earn \$100 million in operating profit after covering all costs.**

**Calculate how many phones it must sell to achieve this target.**

**Then calculate the profit if Apple sells only 300,000 phones. Explain whether your selected pricing strategy is financially attractive.**

### **Slide 8 — Marketing campaign**

**Apple has a \$20 million marketing budget, already included in the \$150 million fixed investment.**

**Divide this budget among at least four activities, such as online advertising, launch events, influencer partnerships, and promotional videos.**

**Show the allocation in a pie chart and explain which activity receives the largest investment and why.**

### **Slide 9 — Final CEO recommendation**

**Present your final recommendation to Apple's CEO.**

**Include your chosen selling price, expected sales, estimated operating profit, three reasons the project should be approved, and the biggest financial risk.**

**Slide 10 \- End with a persuasive closing statement.**

# **Homework 2: Tesla — The Flying Car Investment Decision**

**Scenario: Imagine Tesla is developing its first flying electric car, called Tesla Sky. You are Tesla's executive team, preparing a presentation for investors who must decide whether to finance the project.**

**Presentation: 10 slides, 8–10 minutes.**

### **Slide 1 — Introduce Tesla Sky**

**Create a professional presentation cover.**

**Include the product name, an original slogan, a product mockup or illustration, and your team members' names.**

### **Slide 2 — Explain the innovation**

**Present the Tesla Sky concept.**

**Describe how the vehicle would operate, at least four proposed features, and three differences between Tesla Sky and a regular electric car.**

**Identify one major engineering challenge.**

### **Slide 3 — Business opportunity**

**Explain why Tesla might enter the flying vehicle industry.**

**Present three potential business benefits, two potential disadvantages, and one alternative use for the technology if the flying car is not commercially successful.**

### **Slide 4 — Product pricing**

**Tesla is considering three selling prices.**

| Option | Selling price |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Luxury** | **\$350,000** |
| **Premium** | **\$300,000** |
| **Standard** | **\$250,000** |

**The variable cost of producing and delivering one vehicle is \$160,000.**

**Calculate the contribution per car and contribution margin percentage for each price.**

**Choose the price you would recommend and explain why.**

### **Slide 5 — Investment requirements**

**Tesla estimates the following fixed investment:**

| Investment category | Amount |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Research and development** | **\$500 million** |
| **Factory construction** | **\$300 million** |
| **Safety testing and certification** | **\$150 million** |
| **Marketing and launch** | **\$50 million** |
| **Total** | **\$1 billion** |

**Calculate the percentage allocated to each category.**

**Create a pie chart and explain which investment category is the largest and whether you would change the allocation.**

### **Slide 6 — Break-even challenge**

**Using the selling price selected on Slide 4, calculate how many flying cars Tesla must sell to recover its \$1 billion fixed investment.**

**Show the formula and create a break-even chart.**

### **Slide 7 — Profit target**

**Investors want Tesla Sky to generate \$500 million in operating profit after covering the \$1 billion investment.**

**Calculate how many vehicles must be sold to reach this target.**

**Then calculate the operating profit if Tesla sells only 10,000 vehicles.**

### **Slide 8 — Production capacity problem**

**Tesla's factory can manufacture only 8,000 flying cars annually.**

**Calculate the maximum annual revenue and contribution at full production capacity.**

**Determine whether Tesla can reach the \$500 million profit target in the first year. If not, calculate how much additional production capacity would be required, assuming every additional vehicle can be sold.**

### **Slide 9 — Final investor decision**

**Present a final investment recommendation.**

**Include the proposed selling price, required sales volume, expected operating profit under your chosen sales forecast, major risks, and your recommendation to approve, delay, or reject the project.**

**Slide 10 \- Finish with a convincing statement to investors.**

## **Additional presentation rules**

**Required for both cases**

* **Maximum 10 slides**  
* **At least 3 charts or data visualizations.**  
* **All financial calculations must show the formula and result.**  
* **Each slide should have a clear headline communicating its main message.**  
* **No reading long paragraphs directly from slides.**  
* **Every team member must speak.**  
* **Final recommendations must be supported by numbers.**  
* **Financial assumptions and fictional product concepts must be clearly identified.**  
* **The presentation must tell one, engaging business story. Slides should connect logically, with each one building on the previous slide and leading toward a final recommendation.**  
* **Do not structure your presentation as a question-and-answer exercise. The case questions are guidelines for what to analyze, not questions to copy onto slides and answer individually.**  
* **Use smooth transitions between slides so that the presentation feels like one complete business proposal rather than a collection of separate tasks.**  
* **Keep the main slides clear and visually engaging. The main presentation should highlight key formulas, results, and business implications without overwhelming the audience with numbers.**
